# New to This? TryHackMe and CTFs

Before the house drags you into its family business, a quick word on where you are and how the game works.

## What is TryHackMe?

TryHackMe is a website for learning cybersecurity by doing it. Lessons come in **rooms**, and every room is split into **tasks**, like the one you are reading now. Each task has some text to read and one or more questions underneath. Type your answer into the box under a question and press **Submit**. A correct answer turns green and earns points; a wrong one just lets you try again, with no penalty.

Some questions say **No answer needed**. For those, read the task and press **Submit** (or **Complete**) to mark it done.

## What is a CTF?

CTF stands for **Capture the Flag**. Instead of chasing a cloth flag around a field, you chase a short piece of secret text hidden inside a puzzle: a file, a website, a log, an image. Finding it proves you solved the puzzle.

In this room every flag looks like this:

```
THM{UPPER_SNAKE_CASE}
```

When you find one, copy the whole thing, braces and all, into the answer box. Flags are case sensitive, so copy rather than retype.

## How to approach a challenge

- **Read the task text twice.** The story is full of hints about where to look and which tool to reach for.
- **Look at everything.** File names, dates, odd lines in a log, the source code of a web page. Investigators who skim miss the clue.
- **Take notes.** Write down names, device IDs, dates, and hashes as you find them. Later tasks will ask for them again.
- **Stuck? Step back.** Re-read the task, try a different tool, or talk it through with your team. You can also ask an organizer for a nudge.
- **Hints cost nothing but pride.** Use them if a task offers them.
