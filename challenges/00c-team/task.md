# Assemble Your Investigators

No detective works alone, and nobody wants to be the only person in the room explaining hash functions to a portrait. This is a team-based room: you investigate as a team, you score as a team, and you climb the leaderboard as a team. Set that up now, before you open a single file.

## Step 1: Pick a captain

Gather your teammates (an organizer will tell you how many people per team) and choose one person to be the **captain**. The captain creates the team on TryHackMe. Everyone else joins it.

Also pick a **team name**. Spooky, punny, or dramatic are all encouraged. Keep it friendly: it goes on a public leaderboard.

## Step 2: The captain creates the team

The captain only:

1. Open **Manage account** on TryHackMe (from your profile menu).
2. Open the **Teams** tab.
3. Click **Create a team** and enter your team name.
4. Click **Invite Members**. TryHackMe gives you a share link.
5. Send that link to your teammates: group chat, message, or read it out if you enjoy suffering.

## Step 3: Everyone else joins

1. Make sure you are logged in to your own TryHackMe account. Every teammate needs one.
2. Open the invite link from your captain and accept it.
3. Check **Manage account > Teams** shows your team name.

A few rules from TryHackMe:

- You can only be on **one team at a time**. If you are already on an old team, leave it first.
- A team holds at most 20 people (the organizers will set a smaller limit for tonight).
- Only the team owner (your captain) can remove members. Anyone can leave a team at any time.

## Step 4: Everyone joins this room

Being on the team does not put you in the room. **Each teammate must open this room and click Join Room on their own account.** If you do not, you cannot download the files or submit answers.

Players who are not on a team can still play the room, but their score does not appear on the leaderboard. Do not be that investigator.

## How team scoring works

- **Progress is shared.** When any teammate answers a question correctly, it counts for the whole team, and the room updates so everyone sees it as answered. You do not need to answer every question once per person.
- **The leaderboard ranks teams, not people.** It shows each team's points, and a chart of how they climbed through the evening.
- **Ties go to speed.** If two teams have the same score, the team that finished its final task first ranks higher. Fast teams beat slow teams, so do not dawdle over the canapés.
- **Resets hit everyone.** If any teammate resets their progress in the room, the whole team's progress resets. Points already earned are kept, but the answered questions are wiped. Nobody touch that button.

## Working together

- **Split up, then share.** Different people can dig into different files, but share everything you find. Clues from early tasks unlock later ones.
- **Keep one shared notebook.** A shared doc, a group chat, or a sheet of paper in the middle of the table. Record every name, device ID, date, and hash.
- **Everyone types.** Take turns at the keyboard. The person who has never opened a terminal before should get a turn too.
- **Argue about suspects, not about each other.** It is Halloween, the host is dead, and that is drama enough.
